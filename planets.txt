Mercury is the smallest planet in our solar system and the closest to the Sun. It has no moons and no atmosphere to speak of. A year on Mercury is just 88 Earth days, but a single day lasts about 59 Earth days. Despite being closest to the Sun, it is not the hottest planet.

Venus is the second planet from the Sun and the hottest planet in the solar system, with surface temperatures around 465 degrees Celsius. Its thick atmosphere of carbon dioxide traps heat in a runaway greenhouse effect. Venus rotates backwards compared to most planets, and a day on Venus is longer than its year.

Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only known planet to support life. About 71 percent of its surface is covered in water. Earth has one natural moon, and its atmosphere is composed mainly of nitrogen and oxygen.

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun, often called the Red Planet because of iron oxide on its surface. It has two small moons, Phobos and Deimos. Mars is home to Olympus Mons, the tallest volcano in the solar system. A day on Mars is very close to a day on Earth, at 24.6 hours.

Jupiter is the fifth planet and the largest in the solar system, so big that all the other planets could fit inside it. It is a gas giant made mostly of hydrogen and helium. Jupiter has a famous storm called the Great Red Spot that has raged for centuries. It has at least 95 known moons.

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and is famous for its spectacular ring system made of ice and rock. It is the second largest planet and, like Jupiter, is a gas giant. Saturn is the least dense planet, so light that it would float in water if a large enough ocean existed.

Uranus is the seventh planet and rotates on its side, likely due to an ancient collision. It is an ice giant with a pale blue-green colour caused by methane in its atmosphere. Uranus is the coldest planet in the solar system, with temperatures dropping to minus 224 degrees Celsius.

Neptune is the eighth and farthest planet from the Sun. It is a deep blue ice giant with the strongest winds in the solar system, reaching over 2,000 kilometres per hour. Neptune takes 165 Earth years to complete a single orbit of the Sun.
